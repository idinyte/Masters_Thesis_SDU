\section{Experiment}
The theoretical and practical force transmission of the rack and pinion mechanism will differ due to losses in the system. Therefore, an experiment was devised in order to calculate the efficiency of the system. In theory, without taking any losses into account, the linear force produced by the rack and pinion is equal to the applied torque divided by the pitch radius of the spur gear, Eq. \ref{eq:rack_and_pinnion_force}.

\begin{equation}
  F_{\text{theo}} = \frac{\tau_m}{r_p}
  \label{eq:rack_and_pinnion_force}
\end{equation}

\noindent
The designed spur gear has the following parameters:

\begin{table}[ht]
  \setlength{\abovecaptionskip}{6pt}
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|}
  \hline
  \multicolumn{1}{|c|}{\textbf{Element}} & \textbf{Symbol}                  & \textbf{Value} \\ \hline
  Module              & m               & 1        \\ \hline
  Number of teeth              & Z               & 12        \\ \hline
  Pressure angle              & $\alpha$               & 20        \\ \hline
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Spur Gear Parameters}
  \label{table:spur_gear_parameters}
\end{table}

\noindent
From these parameters the pitch radius can be calculated Eq. \ref{eq:pitch_radius}

\begin{equation}
  r_{p} = \frac{m \cdot Z}{2} = 6mm = 0.006m
  \label{eq:pitch_radius}
\end{equation}

\noindent
The experimental setup can be seen in Fig. \ref{fig:experimental_setup_load_cell_connection_diagram}. The experiment aims to measure the actual torque provided by the motor and the transmitted linear force. The Dynamixel motor is controlled using a computer and U2D2 communication converter. It is controlled using current control operation mode. Positive current opens the gripper, and negative current closes it. The goal current for this motor can only be incremented in 2.69 [mA] intervals. The current is converted to the torque using Eq. \ref{eq:current}. The stall torque obtained from this motor's data sheet is 4.1 [Nm] (at 12.0 [V], 2.3 [A]). The torque constant can be calculated as $k_t = \tau / I = 4.1 / 2.3 = 1.783 $. The force is measured using a 1kg load cell placed in one of the finger holes of the exoskeleton. The load cell sensor data is being read by the Aduino Uno and transmited to the computer. The load cell was calibrated against a known weight object (a smartphone). As with all measurement devices, there can be some measurement errors. The zero balance uncertainty is $\pm $0.1\% rated output (RO), the temperature effect on zero is $0.02\% \text{RO}/^\circ\text{C}$, the creep is $0.1\% \text{full scale (FS)}/5\text{min}$, repeatability error is $0.03\% \text{FS}$, and deviation from an ideal linear response is $0.05\% \text{FS}$.

\bigskip \noindent
The obtained experimental values and theoretical values are plotted in Fig. \ref{fig:experiment_results}. In order to be able to read the plot left to right instead of right to left, as all of the values are negative, the x-axis and y-axis in the plot are inverted. Using this data, the efficiency of the exoskeleton can be calculated using Eq. \ref{eq:efficiency}. The efficiency was calculated as an average over the values starting from the point where the exoskeleton exhibits non-zero force Eq. \ref{eq:efficiency_average}.

\begin{equation}
  \eta = \frac{F_{\text{exp}}}{F_{\text{theo}}} \times 100\%
  \label{eq:efficiency}
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
  \eta_{\text{avg}} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=6}^{n} \left( \frac{F_{\text{exp},i}}{F_{\text{theo},i}} \times 100\% \right) = 56.93\%
  \label{eq:efficiency_average}
\end{equation}
